Distance estimate: RSSI + accelerometer (experimental)

Distance from the RSSI: d = 10(P0 − RSSI) / (10 n), P0 the RSSI at 1 m. A single reading is off by several dB (fading, hand, body), so the RSSI has to be averaged, which only works while the distance doesn't change. The accelerometer tells when it does: gravity removed, the acceleration is integrated into a speed, reset to 0 each time the ring is still. It can't give the distance itself (no gyroscope or compass: the direction of the other end is unknown), but the length of the move bounds how much the distance may have changed. "Fused": a Kalman filter that averages the RSSI over several seconds while the rings are still, and follows it right away during a move. Compared with the RSSI alone, low-passed over 0.5 s (fast, noisy) and 1 s. Ring to ring: both directions (A hears B, B hears A), and the motion of both rings when both are connected.

Group distance

Two groups of rings, e.g. A: two rings on one hand, B: two rings fixed on a table. The distance uses the mean RSSI (dB) of every link between the groups, both directions: their antennas point different ways, so the mean cancels much of the orientation effect (tests, 2026-11-20: a single link off by up to ×2, the mean of 4 links by ×1.3). Needs ring-to-ring RSSI, and every link between the groups heard. Calibrate it in the table below (row "Group …"), at 2 distances.
