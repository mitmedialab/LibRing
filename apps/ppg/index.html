Heart rate: green on the finger. Red and IR are for SpO2. Auto: the ring's gain control (bright LED + offset DACs); moving the brightness or pulse sets manual mode. The LEDs flash briefly (25 times per second): IR is invisible to the eye and too dim for most phone cameras in normal use. Camera check: the selected LED at maximum for 6 s, look through the phone's front camera (rear cameras usually filter IR).

Each beat is timed on the green pulse (steepest edge, refined by matching the average pulse shape), only while the pulse is clear and the hand still; deformed beats and intervals off by more than 20 % from the recent ones are dropped. RMSSD (beat-to-beat changes) and SDNN (spread) over the last minute. At 24 samples per second the timing noise is ~7 ms per interval: an estimate, which reads a bit high when the variability is low.

Not calibrated, not a medical measurement: ratio of the red and IR pulses (each divided by the light that went through the finger) with the textbook curve 110 − 25 R. Most of the light reaching the sensor leaks inside the ring: the leak calibration measures it (kept in this browser, per ring). The ring shows a value only when the red and IR pulses match (correlation ≥ 90 %).
